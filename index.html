References

Academic sources and materials used throughout this course (APA format).

  1. McCumber, J. R. (1991). Information systems security: A comprehensive model. In Proceedings of the 14th National Computer Security Conference (Vol. 2, pp. 328–337). National Institute of Standards and Technology. https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/conference/1991/10/01/proceedings-14th-national-computer-security-confer/final Used for understanding the McCumber Cube framework and its three dimensions.
  2. National Institute of Standards and Technology. (2017). An introduction to information security (NIST Special Publication 800-12, Rev. 1). U.S. Department of Commerce. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-12r1.pdf Primary source for confidentiality, integrity, availability, and security foundations.
  3. National Institute of Standards and Technology. (2004). Standards for security categorization of federal information and information systems (FIPS Publication 199). U.S. Department of Commerce. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/FIPS/NIST.FIPS.199.pdf Referenced for official definitions of the CIA triad security objectives.
  4. National Institute of Standards and Technology. (n.d.). Confidentiality, integrity, availability. Computer Security Resource Center Glossary. https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/confidentiality_integrity_availability Quick reference for CIA triad terminology while writing Reflection 1.
  5. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency. (n.d.). Cybersecurity best practices. U.S. Department of Homeland Security. https://www.cisa.gov/topics/cybersecurity-best-practices Used to connect classroom concepts to real-world security practices and user behavior.
  6. National Institute of Standards and Technology. (2020). Zero trust architecture (NIST Special Publication 800-207). U.S. Department of Commerce. https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.800-207.pdf Background reading for topics I want to explore further this semester.
