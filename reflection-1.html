Reflection 01

McCumber Cube, Foundations of Security,
and Security Principles

Brian Justine D. Alcoran BSIT — Information Security

New Insights

Before this lesson, I honestly thought cybersecurity was mostly about using antivirus software and making strong passwords. I didn't realize there were actual models that explain how security works. Learning about the McCumber Cube showed me that protecting information isn't just about technology. It also involves the people using the system, the policies they follow, and how information is handled. One thing that stood out to me was the CIA Triad. At first, the terms confidentiality, integrity, and availability sounded complicated, but when our instructor gave examples, they became easier to understand. I realized that information can still be considered insecure even if only one of those three is missing. This topic made me see cybersecurity in a much broader way than I expected.

Concepts I Want to Learn More About

After discussing the topic, I became interested in learning more about ethical hacking and penetration testing. I want to understand how security professionals find weaknesses in systems before attackers do. I also want to learn about cloud security because many companies now store their data online, and I think knowing how to protect cloud-based systems will be useful in the future.

Confusion or Challenges

The McCumber Cube was honestly the hardest part of the lesson for me. During the discussion, I kept mixing up the different parts of the cube because there were several dimensions to remember. It took me some time to understand how the information states, security goals, and safeguards are all connected. I also found it challenging to think about real-life examples for every part of the model. After reviewing my notes and reading about it again, I started to understand it better. I know I'll probably need more practice before I can explain the whole model without looking at my notes, but I think that's normal since this is still a new topic for me.

Personal Reflection

This lesson made me realize that cybersecurity is not only the responsibility of IT professionals. Even ordinary users can help protect information by following good security practices. Simple habits like creating strong passwords, being careful with suspicious links, and keeping personal information private can already make a big difference.

As a BSIT student, I know that understanding these concepts now will help me in the future, especially when I start working in the IT industry. This lesson reminded me that technology alone cannot provide security. It also depends on the people who use it and the decisions they make every day. Even though I'm still learning, I feel that this topic gave me a stronger appreciation of why information security is so important.
